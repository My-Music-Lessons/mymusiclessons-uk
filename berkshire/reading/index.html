ONE CLEAR NEXT STEP

From a first conversation to music in a Reading school.

  1. 01

    One conversation

    You name the school. We find out what is already running there, what the funding allows, and what we could put on next term.

  2. 02

    A room and a slot

    You give us a room and a rotating slot. Nothing else on your timetable changes, and the rotation is what stops one subject losing out.

  3. 03

    We staff it and we cover it

    Our teachers arrive, teach one-to-one, in small groups and in bands of up to five, and we send cover when somebody is ill. Your office is not asked to find anybody.

  4. 04

    The records come back to you

    Registers, practice notes and safeguarding evidence, held by us and open to your team. We bill families directly; you keep the relationship.

WHO PAYS, AND WHEN

Who pays for a place

A place has one payer and never two. Which one applies at a school in Reading depends on that school and its funding, not on who asks first. Ask and we will tell you what is available at yours.

  • Funded places available

    A school funds a number of places, so those children pay nothing. How many depends on the school and its funding.

  • Family-paid places

    A flat monthly plan, the same every month, with missed lessons made up rather than refunded.

The music service for Reading

A music service is the whole thing, not a teacher: staffing, cover when somebody is ill, instruments, records of who played what, and one invoice. That is what we run in Reading.

What a Reading school provides is a room and a rotating slot. Where it funds places it pays for those, and there is nothing else - no cover to arrange, no instruments to buy, no invoices through your office.

In-school music service in Reading

In school means in the building, in the timetable, in term time. Our teachers hold a room in Reading schools and children come to them between lessons.

Children here learn Piano, Guitar, Drums, Singing and Ukulele, and the mix is settled by what they ask for in the first term rather than by us.

School music lessons in Reading

Peripatetic music teachers in Reading

The word is old and the arrangement is simple: the same teacher comes to your Reading school every week, knows the children by name, and belongs to a round rather than to a booking.

Instrumental tuition in Reading

Tuition rather than an activity: there is a thing to learn, a way of telling whether it is being learnt, and a note home saying what to practise before next week. That is what a Reading family is paying for, or what a Reading school is funding on their behalf.

What a Reading school provides is a room and a rotating slot. Where it funds places it pays for those, and there is nothing else - no cover to arrange, no instruments to buy, no invoices through your office.

Music lessons for Reading schools

For a school this is provision without administration: we staff it, cover it, record it and bill the families directly. Reading schools keep their timetable and lose the paperwork.

Reading is in Berkshire, and the agreement is the same in every school we work with there.

School band and ensemble in Reading

Up to five children, one room, one piece of music. A band or a small ensemble is where a child stops practising and starts playing with other people, which is the part a lesson on its own cannot give them - and in Reading it happens inside the school day like everything else we run.
