ONE CLEAR NEXT STEP

From a first conversation to music in a Wakefield school.

  1. 01

    One conversation

    You name the school. We find out what is already running there, what the funding allows, and what we could put on next term.

  2. 02

    A room and a slot

    You give us a room and a rotating slot. Nothing else on your timetable changes, and the rotation is what stops one subject losing out.

  3. 03

    We staff it and we cover it

    Our teachers arrive, teach one-to-one, in small groups and in bands of up to five, and we send cover when somebody is ill. Your office is not asked to find anybody.

  4. 04

    The records come back to you

    Registers, practice notes and safeguarding evidence, held by us and open to your team. We bill families directly; you keep the relationship.

WHO PAYS, AND WHEN

Who pays for a place

A place has one payer and never two. Which one applies at a school in Wakefield depends on that school and its funding, not on who asks first. Ask and we will tell you what is available at yours.

  • Funded places available

    A school funds a number of places, so those children pay nothing. How many depends on the school and its funding.

  • Family-paid places

    A flat monthly plan, the same every month, with missed lessons made up rather than refunded.

IN MORE DETAIL

More about the service in Wakefield

The same service, answered the way a different reader would ask about it. A business manager and a music lead are not asking the same question, and neither of them should have to read past the other one’s answer to find their own.

The music service for Wakefield

A teacher is one person on one day. A service is what happens when that person is ill, when a child moves up a year, when a parent asks what their money bought, and when somebody asks the school to account for it. We hold all four for Wakefield schools.

In Wakefield that covers 53 primary schools, and a head looking for their own will find it listed on this page.

In-school music service in Wakefield

In school means in the building, in the timetable, in term time. Our teachers hold a room in Wakefield schools and children come to them between lessons.

A lesson in Wakefield is one to five children, in term time, inside the school day, and a practice note goes home after it.

School music lessons in Wakefield

A lesson in Wakefield is one child, or two or three learning the same thing alongside each other, or up to five playing one piece together. Which of those a child gets depends on the child, not on what is cheapest to run.

What a Wakefield school provides is a room and a rotating slot. Where it funds places it pays for those, and there is nothing else - no cover to arrange, no instruments to buy, no invoices through your office.

Peripatetic music teachers in Wakefield

Peripatetic means travelling, and the travelling is the point. A teacher on a fixed round through Wakefield has somebody else on that round already working in the next school along, so a missed week is covered rather than refunded.

When a teacher on the Wakefield round is ill, the person who covers is already working in the next school along.

Instrumental tuition in Wakefield

Tuition rather than an activity: there is a thing to learn, a way of telling whether it is being learnt, and a note home saying what to practise before next week. That is what a Wakefield family is paying for, or what a Wakefield school is funding on their behalf.

The 53 primary schools listed for Wakefield come from the Department for Education register rather than from us.

Music lessons for Wakefield schools

What we ask of a Wakefield school is deliberately narrow - a room and a rotating slot - and everything after that is ours. No invoices through your office, no cover to find, no instrument cupboard to manage.

Wakefield is in West Yorkshire, and the agreement is the same in every school we work with there.

School band and ensemble in Wakefield

Up to five children, one room, one piece of music. A band or a small ensemble is where a child stops practising and starts playing with other people, which is the part a lesson on its own cannot give them - and in Wakefield it happens inside the school day like everything else we run.

Children in Wakefield learn Piano, Guitar, Drums, Singing and Ukulele, and the mix is settled by what they ask for in the first term rather than by us.
